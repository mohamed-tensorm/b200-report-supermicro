Supermicro B200 node s946907x5206414: progressive stress assessment and GPU 4 failure analysis

Eight-GPU NVIDIA B200 node evaluated with the Tensor Machines progressive stress recipe across four assessment runs on 2026-10-08 and 2026-10-09 (UTC). Seven GPUs behaved uniformly and within expectation. GPU 4 was thermally abnormal at idle before any load, ran at its minimum clock under every workload, and left the PCIe bus during the memory-bandwidth phase of the full assessment. The node then required a reboot and did not return.

Executive assessment

GPU 4 (97:00.0)
Failed — not serviceable in current state
Thermally abnormal at idle in every snapshot (71–82 °C, 220–239 W at 0 % utilization vs 33–43 °C, 188–205 W for peers). Ran at 120 MHz with hardware thermal slowdown under all load. Delivered 5–6 % of peer throughput. Dropped off PCIe slot 105 at 06:01:44 UTC.
GPUs 0–3, 5–7

GEMM 1,177–1,229 TFLOPS FP16 (±2.2 %), HBM 6.50/7.57/6.58 TB/s read/write/copy, zero ECC, no thermal throttling, cooldown to idle within 60 s. Power-cap-bound at the 1,000 W limit — expected for B200.
Node
Down — reboot required, did not return
After GPU 4 left the bus, the driver flagged all eight GPUs “Node Reboot Required” (Xid 154). New CUDA contexts failed for the rest of the run. The node did not come back after reboot. No post-failure measurements exist.
Assessment coverage
Baseline tier partially completed; Tier 2/3 not reached
Completed: idle baseline, warm-up GEMM, 1A GEMM, 1C inference (×2 runs), 2A inference (×1 run, 7 GPUs). 1B ran to completion on 7 GPUs but the phase is recorded as failed. 2B–2E, 3A, 3B and NVLink were never executed.

Major strengths (seven peer GPUs)

  • Compute uniformity. Sequential FP16 GEMM sustained 1,176.7–1,228.8 TFLOPS across the seven GPUs; each GPU repeated its warm-up result within 0.2–0.8 % in the later 1A phase despite a different thermal context (concurrent vs sequential).
  • Memory subsystem. HBM read 6,495–6,510 GB/s, write 7,566–7,569 GB/s, copy 6,572–6,584 GB/s (six GPUs reported before the phase aborted) — a spread of 0.2 %. Zero ECC correctable or uncorrectable errors on every GPU in every run; zero remapped rows.
  • Integrated inference. 1C (Llama-3-8B INT8) 15,297–15,427 tok/s per GPU with 0.8 % spread; 2A (Llama-2-13B INT8, FP8 KV) 4,052–4,179 tok/s with 3.1 % spread and ITL p99 44.7–45.4 ms. Silent-data-corruption probe: 100 % cross-GPU agreement.
  • Thermal headroom. Peak peer temperatures 62–75 °C under 1,000 W load, 45–51 °C below the slowdown limit at idle; return to idle temperature within 60 s of load removal; idle-to-idle drift ≤ 1 °C.

Material concerns

  • GPU 4 was abnormal before any load and in every subsequent idle window: 35–40 °C hotter than its neighbours at 0 % utilization with identical inlet air (33 °C) and fan speed. Its driver counters show 10.5 h of accumulated thermal slowdown before our first archived run.
  • GPU 4 failed hard, not gracefully. No ECC, PCIe replay, or AER precursor appears in the available logs; the transition from “slow but running” to “off the bus” was abrupt (one 0.5 s telemetry interval).
  • Blast radius was the whole node. A single GPU leaving the bus made all eight GPUs require a node reboot and blocked every subsequent CUDA workload. The node then failed to return — root cause of the non-return is not measured.
  • Chassis fan response. System fans held ~11.6 k RPM throughout every run and did not increase while the HGX board reported a GPU 19–30 °C above the others and a negative thermal-limit margin. Whether the fan policy is expected on this platform is a vendor question.
  • BMC sensor MB_FAN2 read 0 RPM with status cr (critical) in all four captures. It may be an unpopulated header; it is listed here because it is a standing critical sensor state on the node.

Deployment recommendation

Do not return GPU 4 to service in its current state. The evidence — elevated idle temperature with 0 % utilization over at least 16 hours, 11.1 h of cumulative thermal slowdown, 5–6 % of peer throughput, and a PCIe link-down event — is consistent across four independent runs and three independent sources (NVML, BMC HGX sensors, kernel log). It supports vendor inspection of the GPU 4 module, its thermal interface/cold-plate contact, and its PCIe path (root port 96:00.0, slot 105). Whether that results in a reseat, thermal rework, or module replacement is a vendor determination; the data does not distinguish between a module-internal fault and a mounting/contact fault.

The seven peer GPUs are deployable once the node is restored, with no workload restrictions indicated by the data. Their Tier 2 and Tier 3 behaviour (training write path, boundary probes, NVLink) is untested, not passed — see deployment guidance.

The node itself is not deployable until the non-return after reboot is diagnosed. This report has no data on that event.

Recommended next actions

  1. Pull BMC SEL and Redfish logs (not available to us) for 2026-10-08 14:00 → 2026-10-09 12:00 UTC. This is the most direct way to confirm or rule out a PCIe/power event on GPU 4 and to explain the failed reboot.
  2. Inspect GPU 4 and its path: module seating, thermal interface and cold-plate contact, any local airflow obstruction, PCIe slot 105 / root port 0000:96:00.0 and any retimer in that path.
  3. Collect nvidia-bug-report and GPU 4 InfoROM/XID history if the device can be enumerated again; compare its idle temperature against peers before any load.
  4. Explain the standing MB_FAN2 = 0 RPM (cr) sensor and confirm the platform fan-control policy under a hot-GPU condition.
  5. After repair, re-run the full recipe starting with a 2 min idle baseline: GPU 4 (or its replacement) should idle within a few degrees of its neighbours and complete 1A at 1,170–1,230 TFLOPS. Then complete Tier 2/3 and NVLink for all eight GPUs.

F1 · GPU 4 was thermally abnormal at idle, before any load, in every capture

Severity: criticalConfidence: high — 5 snapshots, 3 sources
Phase 0 Idle Baseline (all runs), provisioning snapshotSubsystem Thermal / powerRuns provisioning, R1–R4

At 0 % utilization and 120 MHz, GPU 4 read 71–82 °C junction and 72–77 °C HBM, drawing 220–239 W, while its seven peers read 33–44 °C and 188–206 W under the same 32–34 °C inlet and the same fan speed. The driver reported a thermal-limit margin of 6–12 °C for GPU 4 versus 45–51 °C for peers. The BMC’s HGX board sensor (maximum GPU temperature across the board) tracked GPU 4 exactly (77 °C at idle). The condition persisted across 16 h and through 7 h idle gaps between runs, so it is not residual heat from a prior workload.

Why it matters. An idle device 35–40 °C above peers with ~35 W extra idle draw is the signature expected from a heat-removal problem (interface, contact, or local airflow) or a module-internal fault. This was detectable with a two-minute idle snapshot — no stress workload was required to flag GPU 4 as unfit for load. It is the single highest-value observation in this assessment.

Evidence: idle temperature chart · per-run idle table · *_nvml_metadata.txt (T.Limit, HBM temp) · provision/26-10-08_14-20_run_provision_and1C.log line ~207

F2 · Under load GPU 4 pinned at its minimum clock with hardware thermal slowdown and delivered ~6 % of peer throughput

Severity: criticalConfidence: high — 3 workloads, 2 runs
Phases 1 warm-up GEMM, 2 1A GEMM, 4 1C inferenceSubsystem Compute, clocks, throttlingRuns R1, R4

Within two seconds of load the junction rose from 78 to 89 °C and the graphics clock fell from a momentary 855 MHz to 120 MHz, where it stayed for the duration of every workload. Any job placed on it would have run at a few percent of expected speed while reporting 100 % utilization — a condition that scheduler-level health checks (utilization, ECC, XID) would not catch. The SDC probe showed its outputs remained correct.

5 · Methodology

Benchmark methodology

Purpose

The recipe treats a GPU node as a set of subsystems — compute, HBM, power delivery, thermal path, interconnect — each of which degrades with a distinct signature. Instead of a single pass/fail, it collects a fingerprint: how each GPU behaves relative to its peers and to itself across phases of increasing demand. The fingerprint is the input to later health classification; the first measurement establishes the reference.

Three tiers

  • Baseline (Tier 1) — 1A GEMM isolates compute; 1B isolates HBM; 1C is the first integrated workload (real LLM inference). Must be highly repeatable so that a 1–2 % move is meaningful.
  • Diagnostic (Tier 2) — larger model inference (2A), training (2B, the only write-heavy workload), convolution, FFT and random-access microbenchmarks. Creates real thermal and memory pressure where degraded devices separate from healthy ones.
  • Boundary probe (Tier 3) — near-limit memory pressure (3A) and sustained power (3B) to observe how gracefully the device behaves near limits. Not health classifiers; failure-proximity indicators.

Why both isolated and integrated workloads

Microbenchmarks attribute a change to one subsystem but miss interactions. Model workloads exercise everything at once but cannot say what moved. Running both, in a fixed order, makes which phases move diagnostic: HBM degradation appears in 1B/2A/2B with 1A normal; compute degradation in 1A/1C/2A with 1B normal; thermal-interface and power-delivery problems in 3B and the cooldown curves; NVLink only in the collective test.

Fixed ordering and telemetry windows

Phases always run in the same order with idle and cooldown observation between them, because thermal history is itself a repeatable signal. Application metrics (TFLOPS, GB/s, tokens/s) come from the workload; temperature, clocks, power, utilization and ECC come from NVML at ~2 Hz for every phase including the idle ones, which have no application output.

What this assessment can and cannot conclude

  • It can state the measured behaviour of each GPU under the executed phases, peer-relative divergence, and run-to-run repeatability where a phase ran twice.
  • It can establish the sequence and timing of the GPU 4 failure from three independent clocks.

The PCIe hypothesis: what is confirmed and what would confirm the rest

Confirmed by host-side evidence

  • The PCIe hot-plug controller at root port 0000:96:00.0 observed the link to slot 105 go down and the card become “not present” at 06:01:44.
  • The NVIDIA driver’s Xid 79 was triggered by that hot-plug interrupt (irq/123-pciehp), 6 ms later.
  • GPU 4’s NVML telemetry stops at 06:01:43.722 — the same second.
  • At the moment of loss the GPU was at 96 °C under load with a hardware thermal-slowdown flag active, and had been within ~6 °C of that temperature for ~3 minutes.

Not determinable from host-side evidence

  • Whether the link dropped because the GPU module stopped responding (e.g. a protective shutdown or GSP/firmware halt), because of a board power event, or because of the slot, connector or retimer.
  • Whether any PCIe correctable errors preceded the drop (no AER output; replay counters not sampled during the run).
  • Why the node did not return after reboot.

What would confirm or rule it out

  1. BMC SEL and Redfish logs for 2026-10-09 05:55–06:05 UTC and for the reboot: a GPU over-temperature or power-fault event, a PCIe/retimer event, or a hot-plug event in the SEL would each point to a different cause. Not measured

  3. Post-event lspci -vvv / nvidia-smi -q for 97:00.0: whether the device re-enumerates, at what link width/speed, with what replay count and InfoROM XID history.
  4. Physical inspection of the GPU 4 module: seating, thermal interface, cold-plate contact, local obstruction; and of the slot 105 path.
  5. Repeat the idle baseline after any intervention: a healthy GPU 4 should idle within a few degrees of GPUs 5 and 6.

9 · Integrated workload behaviour

Integrated versus isolated workloads

Two integrated LLM workloads ran (1C twice, 2A once) alongside two isolated microbenchmarks (GEMM twice, HBM bandwidth once). Together they allow three observations that neither class alone would support:

  • GPU 4’s problem is not compute-specific or memory-specific. It appeared identically in the pure-compute GEMM (6 % of peers), the pure-memory bandwidth test (4 % memory-controller utilization), and the integrated inference workload (6 % of peer tokens/s). The common factor in all three is the 120 MHz thermal clamp. In the recipe’s diagnostic logic, “everything moves together, with thermal flags” points to the thermal path or power delivery, not to the HBM or tensor cores.
  • Peer behaviour under integrated load matches isolated load. Inference drove peers to 647–758 W and 1,460–1,630 MHz with the same 0.8–3.1 % uniformity seen in GEMM and bandwidth. No GPU became an outlier only under combined load — the signature the recipe would flag as a latent interaction problem is absent.
  • Integrated workloads tolerated the slow device; the isolated bandwidth test is where it died. vLLM simply produced fewer tokens on GPU 4. The bandwidth kernel kept GPU 4 at 100 % utilization and 93–96 °C for three minutes before the link dropped. This is consistent with sustained near-limit temperature being the proximate stress, but the data cannot show that another workload would not have produced the same outcome.

Silent-data-corruption probing in both 1C runs (3 repetitions × 64 tokens per GPU, cross-GPU majority vote) showed 100 % agreement for all GPUs, including GPU 4 in run 1. GPU 4 produced correct results slowly; it did not produce wrong results.

10 · Boundary behaviour

Behaviour near operating limits

Tier 3 boundary probes did not execute

3A (near-limit inference) was skipped by configuration; 3B (sustained power) and NVLink failed to start after the GPU 4 event. No GPU on this node has been characterized near its memory or sustained-power boundary. The statements below concern limits that were encountered, not probed.

Peers — the power limit

The only boundary the seven peers reached was the 1,000 W power cap, under GEMM and HBM load. The transition was gradual and controlled: clocks settled at 985–1,114 MHz (GEMM) or 1,949 MHz (bandwidth) with the SW-power-cap flag set, throughput was flat (per-second TFLOPS standard deviation 0.4–1.0 %; one GPU, GPU 1, showed a brief 1,334 TFLOPS spike), and temperature stayed 25–30 °C below the slowdown limit. After load removal they returned to idle temperature within 60 s. This is the expected behaviour of a healthy B200 under a tensor-core power virus.

Practical constraint identified: none beyond the configured power limit. Repeatability of this limit behaviour across the two GEMM exposures was within 0.8 %.

GPU 4 — the thermal limit

GPU 4 reached its thermal slowdown limit before load (T.Limit margin 6–12 °C at idle) and crossed it within two seconds of any load. The transition was abrupt (clock 855 → 120 MHz in 8 s) and total: the device stayed at the floor for every loaded second thereafter. Temperature nevertheless continued to climb at the floor clock (88 → 96 °C over the 1B exposure), indicating the clamp could not hold the junction below the limit. The eventual exit was a hard PCIe link loss, not a graceful shutdown. No recovery or cooldown behaviour is available.

Practical constraint identified: GPU 4 has no usable operating envelope in its current state.

11 · Findings beyond pass/fail

What a conventional report would have missed

A device that was anomalous before any load

A pass/fail burn-in starts the clock when the stress begins. The decisive evidence here — 35–40 °C excess idle temperature, 35 W excess idle draw, 10.5 h of pre-existing thermal-slowdown time — was all available at 0 % utilization. The two-minute idle baseline in the recipe exists for exactly this reason.

100 % utilization at 6 % output

GPU 4 reported 90–100 % GPU utilization throughout 1C and GEMM while delivering ~6 % of peer throughput. Utilization, ECC and XID — the usual health-check trio — were all “fine” until the moment the device vanished. Only clock, temperature, throttle reason and peer-relative throughput exposed the condition.

Hard failure without the recipe’s usual precursors

ECC counts, bandwidth droop and clock drift are the degradation signatures the recipe is built to catch. GPU 4 showed none — it never ran un-clamped long enough to show them. The lesson for the methodology: idle thermal margin and thermal-slowdown counters belong in the pre-assessment gate, ahead of any load.

Peer fingerprints are stable and small

GPU 2 is reproducibly ~2.5 % below the peer mean on GEMM and GPUs 3 and 5 reproducibly highest; GPUs 0 and 3 run ~10 °C cooler than the others in every run. None of this is a defect; all of it is the baseline a future assessment should be compared against. A pass/fail report discards it.

The node, not the GPU, is the unit of failure

One device leaving the bus produced Xid 154 on all eight, a UVM fatal error, and a reboot requirement; the node then did not return. Capacity planning that counts this as “7 of 8 GPUs available” is wrong for this platform/driver combination.

Chassis cooling did not react

Fans stayed flat while the HGX board reported a negative thermal margin. Whether by design or not, a per-GPU thermal fault on this platform is not mitigated by the chassis. That is relevant to how aggressively a cluster should fence a GPU that idles hot.

  • R1 and R2 1C configurations differ (batch 1024 × 1 instance × 420 s vs 448 × 2 × 180 s); they are not a repeatability pair.
  • The 2A metadata’s method_deviation_note references vLLM 0.5.4 pins; the container actually ran vLLM 0.29.0. The note describes the reason for GPTQ-over-AWQ, not the executed stack.
  • BMC STATUS_* columns are hex sensor codes (C0h…), constant across all runs.
  • The industry example report in the source folder (Velerity, 2026-06-17) describes a different machine (an A100 DGX); it is used only as a format reference in §14.

Metric definitions

Sustained TFLOPS
Mean FP16 tensor-core throughput over the scored window of a GEMM run, excluding warm-up iterations. 1 TFLOPS = 10¹² floating-point operations per second.
Tokens/s, TTFT, ITL
Inference throughput per GPU per second; time-to-first-token per prompt; inter-token latency percentiles per prompt (p50/p95/p99). Scored window excludes the configured ramp (60 s or 120 s).
Throttle reason (NVML bitmask)
1 = GPU idle · 4 = SW power cap · 8 = HW slowdown · 32 = SW thermal slowdown · 64 = HW thermal slowdown. 104 = 8 + 32 + 64.
T.Limit margin
nvidia-smi -q “GPU T.Limit Temp”: degrees of headroom remaining before the thermal slowdown threshold. Negative means over the limit.
Clocks Event Reasons Counters
Cumulative microseconds the GPU has spent in each slowdown state since driver load.
Xid
NVIDIA driver error-event code written to the kernel log. 31 memory page fault · 79 fallen off the bus · 154 recovery action changed.
HGX_GPU_Temp / HGX_T-Limit (BMC)
HGX baseboard aggregate sensors as exposed over IPMI; behave as the maximum GPU temperature and minimum thermal margin across the board.
Peer band
In the timeline charts, the shaded region spans the minimum to maximum of GPUs 0–3, 5–7 at each time step; the line inside it is their median.

How this report goes beyond a conventional burn-in report

The industry example supplied with the source material is a one-page vendor burn-in report: asset identity, component inventory, a 30-minute stress run summarized as maximum/average temperature, power and fan speed, a checklist of health checks (ECC 0, XID 0, NVSM healthy), and a single PASS. It is a competent instance of standard practice. Applied to this node it would have had to answer one question — did it break? — and GPU 4 would have answered it. What follows is what the conventional format could not have said.

Question an operator asksConventional burn-in reportThis assessment
Did it break?“FAIL — XID 79 on GPU 4.”Same event, placed in a 16-hour timeline with three independent clocks (NVML, BMC, kernel), the exact phase and second, and the kernel’s PCIe hot-plug evidence that precedes the Xid.
Was anything wrong before it broke?Not addressed — stress starts the clock.Yes: 71–82 °C idle, +35 W idle, 10.5 h of pre-existing thermal slowdown, Xid 31. Detectable in a two-minute idle snapshot.
How hot does it run?Max/avg GPU temperature for the run (would read 96 °C max — dominated by GPU 4, saying nothing about the other seven).Per-GPU idle and peak temperatures, HBM temperature, thermal-limit margin, positional stratification among peers, cooldown decay curves, idle-to-idle drift, and chassis fan response.
Is performance right?Not measured.Quantified per subsystem and per GPU: GEMM, HBM read/write/copy, two inference scales with latency percentiles, with uniformity bands; GPU 4 at 6 % of peers while reporting 100 % utilization.
Are the GPUs consistent with each other?Not addressed.Central to the method: ±2.2 % GEMM, 0.2 % HBM, 0.8–3.1 % inference among peers; one extreme outlier; stable per-device fingerprints (GPU 2 low, GPUs 3/5 high; GPUs 0/3 cool).
What limits the machine?Not addressed.Peers: the 1,000 W power cap, reached gracefully. GPU 4: the thermal limit, reached before load. Memory and NVLink boundaries explicitly marked untested.
Is the behaviour repeatable?Not addressed.GEMM repeated within 0.8 % per GPU across two exposures; GPU 4’s condition reproduced in five snapshots; single-run limits declared where a phase ran once.
What did the failure do to the rest of the node?Not addressed.Xid 154 on all eight GPUs, CUDA unavailable node-wide, UVM fatal error, BMC HGX sensors blank, node did not return — with the peers shown to be unaffected in performance and temperature.
What should I do next?“—” or “RMA”.Ranked actions with the evidence each would add: SEL/Redfish review, physical inspection of GPU 4 and slot 105, fan-policy confirmation, re-baseline criteria, and the specific signals to monitor fleet-wide.
How confident is each statement?Binary.Severity and confidence stated separately per finding; claims made to the vendor verified individually, including a unit error we introduced ourselves.

The conventional report answers “ship it?” for a point in time. This assessment additionally produces the per-GPU, per-subsystem fingerprint that makes future change measurable — which is the recipe’s purpose as the input to a health passport.
